FROM A PERSONAL NEED TO A SHARED PROBLEM

Pet care involves a lot to remember.
Keeping track of it shouldn’t.

The starting point

As a pet owner and former veterinary nurse, I saw how easily care information could become fragmented across documents, notes, apps and memory.

I wanted one simple place to keep my cats’ essential information and understand what needed attention next, without turning everyday care into another complicated task.

What I discovered

To understand whether this was only a personal frustration, I spoke with two pet owners about how they managed routines, health information and upcoming care. Although these conversations were exploratory, three recurring problems emerged:

Documents and health information were stored in different places

Upcoming care was easy to forget until it became urgent.

Owners lacked a clear view of what had already happened and what needed attention next.

The challenge wasn’t adding more
information.

arrow_right_altIt was making the right information easier to remember,
access and share.

How might I help pet owners stay on top of everyday care
without making the experience feel overwhelming?

Product goal

  • Understand what needs attention next.
  • Record everyday care with minimal effort.
  • Access essential information when it is needed.

From scattered tasks to one clear care loop

I structured Truffle around a repeatable flow that reflects how everyday care happens:

  1. Choose pet
  2. Go to plan care
  3. See what is next
  4. Complete it
  5. Keep a record

This loop became the foundation for the timeline, reminders, care records and shareable pet summary.

Turning insights into decisions

The conversations with two pet owners helped me identify the main problems and refine how Truffle
should respond to them. These insights were translated into five key product decisions.

DECISION 1

Onboarding that starts
with action

By creating an interactive onboarding, I
helped users understand the app while
creating their first basic pet profile.

  • Faster adoption
  • Easy start

Decision 2

Timeline over navigation

The app is structured around the timeline, making it the main place where everything happens.

Instead of switching between tabs, user can see what happened and what’s coming next. All in one place.

  • Less searching
  • More clarity

Decision 3

Reducing notification fatigue

Not every care task needs to interrupt the user.

I designed notification settings that let people choose which reminders appear as alerts, helping the app stay useful without becoming overwhelming.

  • Fewer interruptions
  • More control

Decision 4

Simplicity over more features

Instead of adding more features, I focused on organizing the app around what pet owners need most often.

This structure prioritizes daily care, pet info and settings, making the app easier to understand from the first session.

  • Clear structure
  • Easier to adopt

Decision 5

Making pet care easier to share

When someone else helps with taking care of a pet, they need quick access to the right and basic routine information.

I designed a simple shareable pet profile with the essential details in one place.

  • Easy handovers
  • Better coordination

Decision 1 of 5

Building Truffle’s identity

It started from a concept

While looking for a name, I came across “trufa”, the Spanish word
commonly used for a dog’s nose because of its resemblance to a
truffle. Translated into English, Truffle became the name of the app.

From Truffle to Truffi

The name inspired Truffi, a playful companion created to guide
pet owners through the app.

  • Emotional connection
  • Friendly guidance
  • Memorable identity

UI Principles

Calm first

Reduce visual noise and avoid
unnecessary urgency.

Clear hierarchy

Make the next action and essential
information easy to identify.

Friendly & functional

Add warmth without compromising
usability.

Reusable patterns

Keep interactions familiar and
consistent throughout the product.

Creating consistency across the experience

To translate the visual direction into the interface, I created reusable patterns for navigation, cards, selectors,
buttons, chips, inputs and icons.

The same structures can support different types of care information while maintaining consistent spacing,
hierarchy and interaction behaviour.

Where Truffle is now

Truffle became a complete high-fidelity prototype,
from the main user flows to the visual identity and
reusable components.

It has not been developed or launched, so I cannot
measure its real impact yet. For now, the result is a
clear product direction that I could continue testing
and improving.

What I would test next

Truffle is still a concept, and the feedback came
from a small group.

My next step would be to test the main flows with
more pet owners, especially people who share pet
care or manage ongoing health conditions.

What I learned

“

I learned that making a product feel simple is not the same as making it basic.
I had many ideas for Truffle, but adding all of them would have made the experience more confusing.

Designing the product and the brand together also showed me that personality works best when it has a purpose. Truffi should guide or reassure the user, not appear everywhere just because it looks cute.

The hardest part was deciding what not to add, and that is probably the most valuable thing I learned from this project.
